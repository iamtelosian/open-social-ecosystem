# Destination-credibility mechanisms in the exit-failure corpus

Status: evidence note, beneficiary-discovery workstream. Companion to exit-failure-retrospectives.md, coded 2026-11-03 in response to issue #2 round 2 (thelumenfield): which destination commitments, if any, can be credible at wedge scale without settlement machinery. This note re-codes the FIXED retrospective corpus - the same ten accounts plus the Zelda contrast, no new case sampling - on one dimension: was any destination-credibility mechanism documented present, and what happened to the destination commitment?

Mechanism classes (from the ch08 8.5 open question): reputation, governance arrangements, independently controlled assets or backups, contracts, redundancy. Coding rule: a mechanism is PRESENT only if the account's source documents it; absence of mention codes ABSENT/UNDOCUMENTED, which is not proof none existed. All accounts remain selected self-reports; this coding inherits every limit of the parent note and adds one: a sustained outcome does not establish which arrangement caused it.

## What the coding distinguishes

**A proposed W1 scoping choice, and its evidence limits.** The corpus contains exactly two mechanism-present cases. Account 10's vendor contract underwrote the artifact carry and nothing else, as reported by the admin: the trust hierarchy, member drift, and email decay were outside the contract and all failed or degraded. Zelda's governance arrangement sits on content licensing plus an external alliance's independence requirement; it covers institutional continuity pressure, not member-level state. The reviewed accounts document no mechanism - settlement or otherwise - covering social state (trust hierarchies, relationships, reachability, discovery), but that is an evidence limit, not a finding: these accounts' silence is not evidence that any mechanism excluded social state, and particular social-state elements may be preservable or measurable by constructions this corpus does not show. On this evidence the defensible scoping CHOICE for a W1 offer is artifact carry (a verified, complete, re-hostable copy) with social-state residue named as out of scope - the one scope where a reviewed account documents a non-settlement mechanism (an ordinary service agreement) at work, noting again that one reported fulfillment does not prove the contract caused it. This is a design choice informed by bounded evidence, not an established conclusion that artifact carry is the only workable scope or that social state is uncoverable.

**A missing premise.** No account mentions a negotiated re-exit right - which is not proof none existed, since the accounts were not collected to answer that question. The only re-exit visible in the corpus is account 1's retreat to the incumbent after the destination deleted the community: involuntary, with the sunk migration cost lost, and observed only as an outcome, not as a complete re-exit process. The wedge card may still reason about re-exit, but it is reasoning about a commitment class the reviewed accounts do not document; it should be carried as a hypothesis, not as a row in a credibility checklist.

**What this does not establish.** Two mechanism-present cases among selected self-reports establish existence, not efficacy, frequency, or causation. Zelda's continuity cannot be attributed to NIWA membership, licensing, or hosting control from the announcement source. Whether reputation, redundancy, or independently controlled assets can make continuity or pricing credible at wedge scale remains uninvestigated in this corpus - the accounts simply do not document them.

## Candidate organizing hypothesis (added 2026-11-04, from issue #2 round 3)

The two mechanism-present codings share a property: the covered commitment's fulfillment is observable to the promisee. Account 10's artifact carry is checkable after migration (post counts, threads, permissions), so a service contract can have acceptance criteria; Zelda's NIWA arrangement polices independent hosting, which is externally observable. Hypothesis (thelumenfield, adopted here as a candidate organizing principle): credibility mechanisms are enforcement technologies that require an observable predicate; the open question for social state is then "which observation," not "which mechanism," and the project's attestation machinery reframes as a candidate observability instrument for particular social-state elements (membership continuity, delivery, reachability), not as settlement.

Limits, kept attached: this is a pattern over two mechanism-present codings among selected self-reports, not an identified explanation. Cost and observability are confounded in the coding (artifact carry may also be the cheapest scope), and these cases cannot separate candidate explanations; that requires evidence about costs, observation and alternatives. Zelda's observable hosting does not make fulfillment of continuity, pricing or social-state promises observable - those are separate predicates the corpus documents no one checking. Named falsifiers (from the same review): a documented credibility mechanism covering a commitment whose fulfillment is not observable to the promisee, or a social-state element already observable via existing protocol signals with no mechanism attached.

## Observation windows, not a stronger one-shot check (added 2026-11-06, issue #2 round 4)

The receiving-side completeness proposal in exit-failure-retrospectives.md is bounded by its observation window. A transfer-time check can provide evidence about the specified preserved items on the receiving side at that time, under the method's coverage and sampling limits. It does not establish later continuity, pricing stability, member retention or social-state fulfillment. These require separately specified observations and windows, not simply a stronger transfer-time check. The organizing question becomes "which observation, over which window, under which assumptions?"

This is a distinction between claim classes, not a claim that every social-state predicate is delayed or that a point observation is irrelevant to future risk. A permission or trust-level mapping explicitly included in the transfer scope may be checkable at transfer time; later drift requires later evidence. A price schedule or governance rule visible at transfer time may inform risk or establish stated terms without proving future fulfillment. Pricing changes can also occur before transfer, as account 7 reports, so timing is attached to the predicate and attempted action rather than assigned permanently to a barrier label.

Proposed instrument requirements, not an existing or tested instrument: each time-extended claim names the promised predicate, observation source, beneficiary-relevant window, sampling cadence or event coverage, missing-observation treatment, and the action a failed or unavailable observation can support. Point observations must not silently become evidence of uninterrupted fulfillment between them. No numeric horizon or cadence is chosen without the promise and beneficiary context; a clean observed window does not guarantee later behavior, and detection does not itself provide recourse or identify a cause.

Thelumenfield's rough 1-2-of-11 transfer-window coding is their interpretation of selected self-reports, not an independently reproduced classification, denominator of attempted exits, failure rate, prevalence estimate or coverage score for a deployed check. The retrospective list also contains multiple predicates within single accounts. Account 11 motivates checking a claimed transfer against the receiver but does not independently prove that a proposed check would catch it, or that silent failure occurs only without such a check. The corpus documents no existing instrument checking the later promises. Finding a later outcome predictable from transfer-time signals could narrow a particular claim under a stated model; prediction is not the same as observing its later fulfillment, and would not by itself collapse these evidence windows.
