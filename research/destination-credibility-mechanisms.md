# Destination-credibility mechanisms in the exit-failure corpus

Status: evidence note, beneficiary-discovery workstream. Companion to exit-failure-retrospectives.md, coded 2026-11-03 in response to issue #2 round 2 (thelumenfield): which destination commitments, if any, can be credible at wedge scale without settlement machinery. This note re-codes the FIXED retrospective corpus - the same ten accounts plus the Zelda contrast, no new case sampling - on one dimension: was any destination-credibility mechanism documented present, and what happened to the destination commitment?

Mechanism classes (from the ch08 8.5 open question): reputation, governance arrangements, independently controlled assets or backups, contracts, redundancy. Coding rule: a mechanism is PRESENT only if the account's source documents it; absence of mention codes ABSENT/UNDOCUMENTED, which is not proof none existed. All accounts remain selected self-reports; this coding inherits every limit of the parent note and adds one: a sustained outcome does not establish which arrangement caused it.

## What the coding distinguishes

**A concrete W1 requirement.** The corpus contains exactly two mechanism-present cases, and they bound what ordinary mechanisms cover. Account 10's vendor contract underwrote the artifact carry and nothing else: the trust hierarchy, member drift, and email decay were outside the contract and all failed or degraded. Zelda's governance arrangement sits on content licensing plus an external alliance's independence requirement; it covers institutional continuity pressure, not member-level state. No account documents any mechanism - settlement or otherwise - covering social state: trust hierarchies, relationships, reachability, discovery. A W1 offer scoped to artifact carry (a verified, complete, re-hostable copy) is the scope at which the corpus shows a non-settlement mechanism (an ordinary service agreement) already working. The wedge card's destination-exposure section already names the social-state harms as outside any bound; this coding shows that is not a gap a mechanism is known to close, settlement or not.

**A missing premise.** "Re-exit rights" as a destination commitment appear in no account. No observed destination was asked for, granted, or denied a negotiated re-exit right. The only re-exit in the corpus is account 1's, and it was involuntary - the destination deleted the community and the moderator retreated to the incumbent, losing the sunk migration cost. The wedge card may still reason about re-exit, but it is reasoning about a commitment class with zero corpus documentation; it should be carried as a hypothesis, not as a row in a credibility checklist.

**What this does not establish.** Two mechanism-present cases among selected self-reports establish existence, not efficacy, frequency, or causation. Zelda's continuity cannot be attributed to NIWA membership, licensing, or hosting control from the announcement source. Whether reputation, redundancy, or independently controlled assets can make continuity or pricing credible at wedge scale remains uninvestigated in this corpus - the accounts simply do not document them.
