# Wedge card W1 - community anchor (candidate, 2026-10-31; revised same day: payer restated as hypothesis; preservation scope marked as proposed requirement; stop condition decomposed and willingness clause scoped; gate split W1a/W1b and verification surface from thelumenfield; smallest-unit closing sentence scoped to evidence-about-this-offer, matching the issue-thread correction)

Status: CANDIDATE HYPOTHESIS, not validated demand and not a commitment to build. Card shape from thelumenfield (issue #2): name the beneficiary, workaround, smallest benefit, payer, stop condition, and exclusions BEFORE any pilot ratification, so the pilot criteria in pilot-success-criteria.md attach to something concrete instead of rationalizing a wedge after the fact. This card is the subtraction artifact that issue asked for. Choosing between this card, an alternate, or neither requires a named evidence pass that is not yet designed; nothing here authorizes one.

## The six fields

**Beneficiary and existing workflow.** The administrator/moderator of one small existing community (order 10^2-10^3 members) already coordinating on a hosted platform. Their workflow: moderation, announcements, member onboarding, archive-keeping - the unglamorous operational loop a community already runs every week.

**Current workaround and why it is inadequate.** Stay put and trust the host; episodic manual exports (non-verifiable, no continuity of identity or member graph); mirror-posting to a second home (diverges, doubles labor). None gives the community a credible group-level exit option, so its insurance value against provider failure or policy change is zero - and the community cannot even discover that until the day it needs it.

**Smallest observable immediate benefit.** A verified, complete, re-hostable copy of the community's own content and membership, produced on a schedule the admin controls, with the group able to check the copy's completeness themselves. "Complete" has to be enumerated before insurance can be claimed as delivered, so the preservation scope is: (a) content - posts, media, moderation records; (b) membership - member identities as portable identifiers; (c) relationships - membership and follow edges; (d) permissions - roles and moderation powers. Explicitly out of scope for a first rehearsal: external dependencies (embeds, bots, integrations), which are named as losses rather than silently dropped. This scope is a proposed requirement to be tested with the beneficiary, not a definition settled by enumeration here - the community rehearsing the copy decides what "complete" must include, and the card's version is the starting proposal. The insurance claim covers only what the copy verifiably preserves; an unverified-preservation rehearsal delivers nothing and counts as a failed rehearsal, not a partial success. Value lands on the first successful rehearsal even if a real exit never happens: the insurance is the product, and a rehearsal is the moment the insurance becomes real rather than asserted. This is the unused-exit-insurance case issue #2 names, pointed at a group rather than an individual. What the group checks, at minimum, is named under "Verification surface" below.

**Payer, if any - hypothesis, untested.** The candidate answer is the community itself: admin time, possibly modest hosting costs for the standby copy. This is a hypothesis about willingness, not an agreed fact; nothing in the project's evidence establishes that any community will pay either cost. No receipt market, no settlement, no external payer assumed. If the benefit cannot clear this payer bar, the wedge fails honestly.

**Stop condition, decomposed - three distinct failure modes, split across two gates (thelumenfield's W1a/W1b, adopted).** The original card coupled tooling and willingness in one full-community rehearsal; that spends a community's trust on unproven tooling. The gate splits: **W1a** is an admin/moderator-only preservation rehearsal over an admin-controlled test space or a consented fixture - it tests (a) technical failure (no verified complete copy is producible) and (b) excessive burden (the copy costs more than an afternoon of admin attention), with no demand claim made or implied. **W1b**, only after W1a passes cheaply, is the community decision and rehearsal - it tests (c) lack of willingness, and only at its own scope: one defined group's informed decline is evidence about this offer under these terms, not about general demand, and stated willingness is not adoption or payment. "Afternoon" means admin attention (active effort), not elapsed wall-clock: a scheduled export that runs overnight can be low burden while failing a literal afternoon clock. A fourth stop, at either gate: making the benefit work requires adding receipt-store, settlement, or conformance machinery - but that falsifies the subtraction premise only if the necessity is demonstrated (the specific benefit component that cannot be delivered without the machinery, named), not merely asserted.

**Explicitly excluded.** Settlement, receipt store, conformance scoring, any payment or token machinery, and any network-scale claim. The card's benefit must stand with none of the paper's measurement layer built.

## Verification surface (minimum, not a receipt store)

"Group can check completeness themselves" needs a named minimum before any rehearsal (thelumenfield's point): (1) a manifest - counts and checksums over preserved content items, so absence is detectable rather than anecdotal; (2) role and permission spot checks by the admin against the source; (3) opt-in member sampling - each participating member checks their own items, burden capped at minutes per member. This is a rehearsal-time artifact, produced for the check and discarded after: it creates no persistent store, no identities beyond the copy itself, and no receipt machinery. If checking completeness honestly requires more than this, that is a finding against the wedge, not a reason to grow the surface.

## Destination exposure (issue #2 round 2, thelumenfield, 2026-11-03)

The card's benefit is a verified maintained copy, not a destination. Round-2 critique: the destination-side failure classes in research/exit-failure-retrospectives.md (governance removal after approval, fragmentation, readiness, pricing instability, infrastructure fragility) mean W1a can pass while a real exit still fails, and W1b willingness measurements conflate tool evaluation with destination-credibility evaluation unless the instrument separates them. The conflation point is adopted with one scoping: any W1b willingness instrument must state the destination assumption it makes rather than let it ride silently, but an unwillingness response is evidence about the stated offer in its stated context - destination belief is one factor among several, not a uniquely identified cause.

What the card does not establish about destination exposure: the maintained-copy construction is an unexecuted hypothesis with unresolved identity, member-graph, permission-preservation, and synchronization questions. It does not establish re-exit without destination cooperation, and it caps nothing: stale or missing data, lost relationships and reachability, discovery loss, and moderation/governance harms all sit outside any current bound. The retrospective account's two lost weeks is a self-reported cost from one migration, not a residual this wedge has measured or achieved.

Which destination commitments could be credible at wedge scale, and by which mechanism, is open. Candidate mechanisms to investigate include reputation, governance arrangements, independently controlled assets or backups, contracts, and redundancy; none are established answers, and credibility is not the same as legal enforceability. If W1b shows unwillingness under a stated offer, that rejection does not by itself demonstrate necessity for settlement machinery - it could reflect many unmet requirements or a badly framed offer. The subtraction premise fails only on a demonstrated necessity that separates those causes; the 8.3 escrow/clawback hypothesis family is one candidate family among several, not the default conclusion of a rejected offer.

## Why this shape and not the individual client

Chapter 6's one firm bootstrapping claim is negative: any plan that treats migration as an individual decision has already failed (6.4), and the day-one-surplus test is measured against the user's existing ritual (6.1). A cheap-second-feed client is the individual-decision shape; it remains on the table as alternate W2 only if paired with a group story. The located usage counts (6.4, migration-evidence-notes.md) remove any license to presume a crew-move population exists, so W1's hypothesis is precisely that a small community with a motivated administrator is the smallest unit at which the paper's coordination claims could be true. If the insurance benefit does not land at the hypothesized smallest unit, the card's own stop-condition scoping applies: the result is evidence about this offer under these terms at that unit, not a general demand verdict - but it removes the strongest named candidate, and no unit where the coordination claim is expected to hold then remains on the table.

## What this card does not establish

Demand (one hypothesis, zero validation), payer willingness, feasibility of group-verified completeness at real-community scale, and the choice between W1, W2, and neither. The evidence pass that would move any of those is unnamed and unscheduled.
