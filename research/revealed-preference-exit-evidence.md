# Revealed-preference vs suppressed-demand evidence pass (author research pass, 2026-10-29)

Bounded pass on 7.5's unchallenged claim (1.5): "protocols exist, users stay, therefore the missing layer is economic machinery" - against its unraised alternative: revealed preference (users may stay because exit is not something they want at any price). One pass, named stopping rule (positioning achieved). What the located evidence does and does not establish for each side, with the residual named.

## Sources

- **Kranz et al. 2023, "Data Portability" (Business & Information Systems Engineering 65:597-607, open access; link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12599-023-00815-w).** Survey of the GDPR right-to-portability status quo. Implementation studies identify three main obstacles: (1) LACK OF USER AWARENESS AND MOTIVATION, (2) providers' reluctance to implement advanced IMPORT solutions, (3) lack of standardization (per Kuebler-Wachendorff et al. 2021, cited therein). GDPR portability scope covers received data, partially observed data (55% of compliant exports in one cited study), and excludes inferred data.
- **Data Transfer Initiative, "Portability in Practice - A Discussion with Google" (dtinit.org/blog/2025/03/03, event summary).** Google's portability APIs now support three use paradigms: one-time transfer (switching), 30-day (trying a new service), 180-day renewable (ongoing parallel use). Source scope: one vendor's API design as reported by an industry initiative; infrastructure maturing, no uptake figures.
- **This paper's own located adoption numbers** (research/adoption evidence pass, 2026-10-26): 0.056% of enumerated ATProto repositories ever migrated; 0.299% of 30-day-active users hold strong identity control (community sources, dated, caveated); located challenger growth episodes are shock/wave-coupled with post-wave recession (-39.8% YoY mobile DAU in one located case).

## What this establishes, and what it does not (interpretation, labeled)

- The revealed-preference alternative has LOCATED PARTIAL SUPPORT: the portability literature itself names insufficient user awareness and motivation as a first-order obstacle, and this paper's own migration counts show exit infrastructure used by a tiny minority even where it exists.
- But the same literature documents a TWO-SIDED failure, not pure revealed preference: provider-side import reluctance and standardization gaps are documented alongside user motivation. Low observed uptake is consistent with BOTH "users don't want exit" and "exit is built to fail." The located evidence does not separate the demand-side and supply-side contributions; no located study measures willingness-to-pay for exit directly.
- The OECD's 2022 portability review (DSTI/CDEP/DGP(2022)12) could not be read in this pass (access-blocked); it may bear on the same question.

## Effect on the 7.5 claim

The missing-layer inference survives this pass as PLAUSIBLE BUT NOT ESTABLISHED: the revealed-preference alternative cannot be dismissed (user motivation is a documented obstacle and measured migration is tiny), and it cannot be confirmed either (supply-side failure is documented in the same literature, and no located study separates the two). The claim stands, unchallenged, with the alternative's evidence status now named: a real, unresolved confound, not a rebuttal and not a confirmation.
