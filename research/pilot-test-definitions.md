# Pilot test definitions - proposals (2026-10-30)

Status: author research pass, one bounded round. These are PROPOSED DESIGNS, not accomplished validation: the stopping rules are proposed thresholds, not validated cutoffs, and nothing here is permission to sample. Each test stays gated until a fixed decision to run it is made. Scope: the three register items awaiting a decision (1.5 crew-move willingness, 3.5 per-role revenue sustainability, A1 adoption deep-dive), framed so each could actually change the paper's claims.

## T-A: Observed group migration outcomes (register 1.5)
What this observes, precisely: already-public, group-documented moves between platforms, coded as arrived-intact, fragmented, or re-formed. That is an observed migration pattern. It is NOT a measure of willingness: willingness is a motive, and movement alone cannot show why a group moved, what members wanted, or what they would do under different pressure. Willingness claims stay in the contestability-reasoning part of 1.5 and are not touched by this test.
Proposed shape: read-only coding over public artifacts of communities that documented a move. No recruitment, no deception, no new posting in those communities.
Proposed stopping rules (proposals, not validated): (a) stop after 12 coded migrations or when no new public case is found in a bounded search window, whichever comes first; (b) any case that turns out to be platform-organized rather than group-organized is coded separately, not discarded.
What 12 or fewer selected cases could legitimately change: selected, self-documented cases cannot establish how common intact moves are, and cannot support any prevalence or willingness claim. The only decisions they can carry are negative and framing ones: if even the documented cases rarely show intact moves, 1.5's empirical-sounding language gets narrowed or dropped back to pure contestability reasoning; if intact moves do appear in the record, 1.5 may say "documented cases exist" and nothing more. This test decides which of those wordings the paper may use - nothing else.

## T-B: Per-role funding-gap map (register 3.5)
What this observes, precisely: for each role the economics chapter names (hosting, witnessing, auditing, interfaces), whether the pool-funding precedents pass documented any funding pattern that covered that role's documented cost drivers. That is a gap map. It is NOT a sustainability finding: a documented precedent shows money was raised for something at least once, not that the role's ongoing costs are covered, that the pattern repeats, or that it scales without a lock-in subsidy. Role-level revenue sustainability cannot be established from these precedents, and this test does not claim to establish it.
Proposed shape: desk analysis only. Pair each role's documented cost drivers with the documented funding precedents (already gathered in the pool-funding pass), and mark each pair: some precedent exists, or no precedent found. No new outreach to operators.
Proposed stopping rules (proposals, not validated): (a) stop when every role has a marked pair or an explicitly recorded gap; (b) a gap is published as an open limit, not filled with a hopeful mechanism; (c) the analysis uses only precedents that already carry sources in the funding pass - new evidence requires its own sourced pass.
What it would change: 3.5's wording moves from "pilot pending" to either "each role has at least one documented funding precedent, with sustainability unestablished" or "these named roles have no documented precedent, an open limit." It cannot move 3.5 to "sustainable."

## T-C: Adoption wording decision (A1, currently parked)
What this observes, precisely: a fixed set of already-public platform adoption series (the same sources as the snapshot pass), read at a fixed cadence over a fixed window, with the snapshot-not-trend framing preserved throughout.
The decision it settles, named in advance: whether the adoption chapter may keep any directional language about open-network adoption, or must stay snapshot-only. That is the only decision on the table. A fixed series over a fixed window can settle a wording decision; it cannot establish an adoption trend, a growth rate, or any causal claim about why numbers moved, and this test does not aim at those.
Proposed stopping rules (proposals, not validated): (a) the series list and the window are fixed in the decision BEFORE any new data is pulled - no series shopping after seeing values; (b) stop at the window's end or when every series is unavailable; (c) overlapping migration events are recorded as temporal overlap, never as shock causation; (d) if the series cannot support the directional wording, the paper says so instead of upgrading the language.
What it would change: A1 unparks into a bounded wording decision, or stays parked with the reason recorded.

## Explicit non-claims
- None of these tests has run. These are proposed designs, not validation of anything. The stopping rules are proposals for ratification, not validated thresholds, and nothing here authorizes sampling, outreach, or spending.
- T-A observes migration outcomes, not willingness. T-B maps funding-precedent gaps, not sustainability. T-C settles a wording decision, not an adoption trend. Each test can change only the named wording of its register item.
- The legal enforceability residual and the independent-challenge gap are separate open items; these tests do not bear on them.
