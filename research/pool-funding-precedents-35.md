# Pool-funding precedents for independent roles (author research pass, 2026-10-29)

Bounded pass narrowing 7.5's unchallenged claim (3.5), following research/revenue-precedents-35.md. That pass located issuer-pays moderation at scale and bundled consumer-paid feed filtering; it left the pool-funded horn of the referee-funding question as "no located market." This pass: one bounded look at pool-funding shapes - pooled donations, quadratic funding, disputant-pays arbitration - plus donated labor as a boundary case. Figures grounded at owning records where located; final settlement stays empirical.

## Located precedents

- **Donation-funded investigative journalism sustains an auditing function at scale (auditors - pooled-donation form).** Pro Publica Inc's most recent Form 990 (fiscal year ending December 2025, IRS data via ProPublica's own Nonprofit Explorer, read 2026-10-29): revenue $74,807,964, of which contributions $71,110,204 (95.1%); expenses $51,294,139. Scope: this funds an auditing function (investigative journalism) whose output is a public good; funders are donors supporting the function, not readers purchasing a referee service for their own consumption. A voluntary pooled-donation shape, not a market. ProPublica is one organization, not a sector; sector-scale figures not located in this pass.
- **Quadratic funding allocates subsidy pools to public goods (rankers/auditors - pooled-crowdfunding + subsidy hybrid).** Gitcoin Grants: over $60M distributed across 20 rounds since 2019 (Gitcoin's GG20 reflections post, read 2026-10-29 - a project self-report). GG20's OSS round: median project raise $1,511, with power-law concentration (10% of funding to the top 0.61% of projects; Gitcoin's own distribution data). Scope: matching pools are funded by protocols and large donors - a subsidy hybrid, not reader-pays; Gitcoin itself notes the median is far below what sustains full-time work.
- **Disputant-pays arbitration pays jurors from case fees (auditors - disputant-pays form).** Kleros: jurors receive arbitration fees in ETH paid by disputing parties; staked PNK redistributes from incoherent to coherent jurors (Kleros documentation FAQ, read 2026-10-29). Scope: the payer is a party seeking a ruling, not the general reader population; this is closer to the issuer-pays horn than to reader-pays. Dispute volume and juror income figures not located in this pass.
- **Boundary case - donated labor substitutes for revenue (moderators - volunteer form).** Wikipedia's content moderation and curation is performed by unpaid volunteers, while the platform is sustained by donations: Wikimedia Foundation FY2023-24 donations and contributions $111.0M, total support and revenue $120.1M (Wikimedia 2023-2024 annual report financials page, read 2026-10-29). Scope: this demonstrates moderation-quality work can be produced at scale without a labor market; it substitutes for referee revenue rather than constituting it, and the donations fund the platform, not the moderators.

## Effect on the 7.5 claim

Narrows the third horn without resolving the claim:
- "No located reader-pays or pool-funded moderation market" narrows to: pooled-donation precedents sustain adjacent auditing functions at tens-of-millions-of-dollars scale (journalism), subsidy-pool mechanisms allocate to public goods with sub-full-time medians (quadratic funding), and disputant-pays arbitration exists at unlocated volume. What remains unlocated in this pass: a precedent where the referee service itself is what readers pay for, at any scale.
- Donated labor (Wikipedia) is a demonstrated substitute for moderation revenue, not an instance of it; it narrows the claim differently - the work can be done, but not necessarily as a paid independent role.
The claim stands, unchallenged. The residual is now named precisely: whether readers will fund referees as a purchased service (rather than support them as charity or perform the work themselves as volunteers) has no located precedent.

## What this pass does not establish

Figures are self-reported (Gitcoin blog) or IRS filings surfaced by the organization's own tool (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer); one pass; volume figures for Kleros and sector-scale figures for nonprofit journalism not located. Nothing here shows these mechanisms fail - they demonstrably exist; the open question is whether any of them maps to per-reader referee funding for social feeds. Final settlement stays empirical (pilot), as before.
