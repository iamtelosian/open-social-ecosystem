# Pool-funding precedents for independent roles (author research pass, 2026-10-29)

Bounded pass narrowing 7.5's unchallenged claim (3.5), following research/revenue-precedents-35.md. That pass located issuer-pays moderation at scale and bundled consumer-paid feed filtering; it left the pool-funded horn of the referee-funding question as "no located market." This pass: one bounded look at pool-funding shapes - pooled donations, quadratic funding, disputant-pays arbitration - plus donated labor as a boundary case. Figures grounded at owning records where located; final settlement stays empirical.

## Located precedents

- **Donation-funded investigative journalism sustains an auditing function at scale (auditors - pooled-donation form).** Pro Publica Inc's most recent Form 990 (fiscal year ending December 2025, IRS data via ProPublica's own Nonprofit Explorer, read 2026-10-29): revenue $74,807,964, of which contributions $71,110,204 (95.1%); expenses $51,294,139. Scope: this funds an auditing function (investigative journalism) whose output is a public good; funders are donors supporting the function, not readers purchasing a referee service for their own consumption. A voluntary pooled-donation shape, not a market. The revenue and contribution shares do not establish how much of this funded the auditing function itself - expenses ($51,294,139) cover all operations. ProPublica is one organization, not a sector; sector-scale figures not located in this pass, and IRS filing figures here are primary records surfaced through an aggregator (Nonprofit Explorer), not independent corroboration.
- **Quadratic funding allocates subsidy pools to public goods (rankers/auditors - pooled-crowdfunding + subsidy hybrid).** Gitcoin Grants: over $60M distributed across 20 rounds since 2019 (Gitcoin's GG20 reflections post, read 2026-10-29 - a project self-report). GG20's OSS round: median project raise $1,511, with power-law concentration (10% of funding to the top 0.61% of projects; Gitcoin's own distribution data). Scope: matching pools are funded by protocols and large donors - a subsidy hybrid, not reader-pays; round distributions do not establish sustainable labor income, and Gitcoin itself notes the median is far below what sustains full-time work.
- **Disputant-pays arbitration pays jurors from case fees (auditors - disputant-pays form).** Kleros: jurors receive arbitration fees in ETH paid by disputing parties; staked PNK redistributes from incoherent to coherent jurors (Kleros documentation FAQ, read 2026-10-29). Scope: the payer is a party seeking a ruling, not the general reader population; this is closer to the issuer-pays horn than to reader-pays. Dispute volume and juror income figures not located in this pass.
- **Boundary case - donated labor substitutes for revenue (moderators - volunteer form).** Wikipedia's content moderation and curation is performed by unpaid volunteers, while the platform is sustained by donations: Wikimedia Foundation FY2023-24 audited financial statements (year ending June 30, 2024, read 2026-10-29): total support and revenue $185.4M, of which contributions of cash and other financial assets $168.2M. Scope: this demonstrates moderation-quality work can be produced at scale without a labor market; it substitutes for referee revenue rather than constituting it, and the donations fund the platform's operations, not the volunteer moderators - Wikimedia-wide donations do not measure moderation revenue.

## Effect on the 7.5 claim

Narrows the third horn without resolving the claim:
- "No located reader-pays or pool-funded moderation market" narrows to four distinct adjacent shapes, none of them a moderation market: donation-funded journalism (an auditing function sustained as charity), subsidy-pool allocation (quadratic funding, with distributions below full-time sustainability), disputant-pays arbitration (Kleros, at unlocated volume), and donated labor (Wikipedia volunteers). What remains unlocated in this bounded pass: a qualifying precedent where readers purchase refereeing as a service.
- Donated labor (Wikipedia) is a demonstrated substitute for moderation revenue, not an instance of it; it narrows the claim differently - the work can be done, but not necessarily as a paid independent role.
The claim stands, unchallenged. The residual is now named precisely: no qualifying precedent located in this bounded pass for reader-purchased refereeing - adjacent funding shapes exist, and whether they map to per-reader referee funding stays an open empirical question, not a market-wide absence.

## What this pass does not establish

Figures are self-reported (Gitcoin blog) or IRS filings surfaced by the organization's own tool (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer); one pass; volume figures for Kleros and sector-scale figures for nonprofit journalism not located. Nothing here shows these mechanisms fail - they demonstrably exist; the open question is whether any of them maps to per-reader referee funding for social feeds. Final settlement stays empirical.

## Instance-operation figures (hosting role; author research pass, 2026-10-30)

The 3.5 question names hosting as one of four roles. This pass pulled instance-published operation figures for the hosting role specifically. In every row the operator and the role coincide, so these rows speak to the COST side of hosting, not to the independent-referee horns above.

- **Fosstodon** (own finances page, https://hub.fosstodon.org/finances/, read 2026-10-30; page undated, period caveat): stated monthly operating costs USD 2,098 (hosting $2,050; DNS $20; Mattermost $15; translation $8; Bitwarden $5). Donation-funded via Patreon and PayPal with a stated six-month running-cost float policy; surplus above costs is donated to open-source projects ($2,162 total 2019-2024, per the page's own ledger). Donation intake figures are not published on the page - the revenue side is a documented gap, not zero. The stated costs are cash line items only; the project's team page describes the people running it, including moderators, as volunteers (https://hub.fosstodon.org/team/, read 2026-10-30).
- **Nivenly Foundation / Hachyderm** (own 2024 financial report, https://nivenly.org/blog/2024/12/31/nivenlys-2024-financial-report/, read 2026-10-30; period calendar 2024): revenue USD 37,520.56; expenses USD 32,667.59; net +USD 4,852.97; closing checking balance USD 38,057.08. Whole-entity figures - the foundation covers Hachyderm plus other projects (Pachli, Haidra), and Hachyderm-only figures are not broken out; the report attributes the roughly 10-12% normalized expense growth primarily to Hachyderm storage and resilience. The foundation's figures are cash only; Hachyderm's own documentation describes its Moderation, Infrastructure and Security teams as volunteers (https://community.hachyderm.io/docs/volunteering/, read 2026-10-30).
- **mstdn.social - two collectives, attribution unresolved.** Two Open Collective collectives both present as this instance, each linking to mstdn.social: "mstdn" (https://opencollective.com/mstdn, point-in-time API row, read 2026-10-30: balance EUR 586,373; recorded yearly income EUR 77,000, API field semantics unverified; 93 backers) and "mstdn-social" (https://opencollective.com/mstdn-social, read 2026-10-30: balance EUR 29,934; zero recorded yearly income; zero recorded backers). Which collective is the operator's primary funding channel could not be established from owning sources in this pass - entity attribution is a documented gap, not a resolved match. Separately, the operator has posted about paying the server bills directly (operator post, https://mstdn.social/@stux/116426680768087810), so at least part of the cost flow runs outside both collectives. No source located establishes whether labor at this instance is paid or volunteer.
- **Documented gaps:** infosec.exchange (search-indexed operating-costs pages now return 404; not located in this pass); mastodon.social (operated by Mastodon gGmbH, whose 2024 annual report is located but whole-entity - software plus operations - so instance-only figures are not separable from it). Active-month user denominators were recorded from each instance's own public API (point-in-time, 2026-10-30), but no per-user ratio is computed: the cost and revenue figures are annual or undated while the denominators are point-in-time, so the periods do not match and a ratio would be a forced estimate.

Scope: four instances, not a sector. What is documented: donation-funded operations (Fosstodon's stated model) and one entity-year cash surplus (Nivenly, calendar 2024). This is NOT a demonstration of ongoing hosting cash sustainability - the rows disagree in period, entity scope and channel completeness, and two of the three carry explicit volunteer-labor exclusions while the third's labor basis is unlocated. Whether donation funding sustains hosting once labor is priced, and whether anything analogous exists for the witnessing, auditing, and interface roles, is not addressed by anything located in this pass.
