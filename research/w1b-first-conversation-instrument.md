# W1b first-conversation instrument: one administrator's present workflow and required preservation scope

Status: proposed instrument, 2026-11-07 - held for review before any use; not fielded, and no conversation has been run or scheduled under it. Source: issue #2 rounds 4-7 (thelumenfield, iamtelosian). Requirements: research/w1b-instrument-requirements.md (R1-R5). Nothing here is a measurement, a recruitment, or consent obtained.

## Purpose and claims boundary

One completed conversation under this instrument can establish:

- Whether a real administrator has a current exit problem (observed report of their present situation, not a hypothetical).
- What preservation scope that administrator actually requires - the first beneficiary test of the wedge card's (a)-(d) proposed requirement, which the card carries as unsettled. Requirements raised unprompted are recorded separately from requirements confirmed only after the checklist is shown, so the checklist cannot silently supply the answer.
- What they say they would do under a stated destination assumption (stated willingness under R4).

It cannot establish adoption, payment, behavioral follow-through, that the required scope is buildable at acceptable cost, or anything about administrators in general. n=1 is one administrator's account, reported under this instrument's framing.

## Consent wording (read first, verbatim or adapted to the channel)

"This is a research conversation about how your community's content and member state is currently kept, and what a move would need to preserve. It asks for no live data, no access to your community or accounts, and no commitment from either side - talking does not sign you up for anything, and declining anything costs nothing. You can skip any question or stop at any point. Notes are attributed by a handle you choose, or kept anonymous. Nothing about you or your community is published without you reviewing it first."

## Question set

**A - Present workflow (observed behavior, not hypothetical).** Current platform and role; community size band; how a copy of the community's content and member state is kept today, if at all; the last time a copy was needed or wished for, and what happened. Record what they actually do, distinct from what they say they value.

**B - Problem currency.** Is there a current reason to move? If not current: when was the last one, and what triggered it? A past problem is dated evidence; only a current one makes this conversation a beneficiary test rather than another retrospective account.

**C - Required preservation scope, elicited open first.** Before showing any list: for your community, what would a copy need to carry for you to call it complete? What would you be unwilling to lose? Record the unprompted requirements verbatim. Only then show the wedge card's proposed scope - (a) content: posts, media, moderation records; (b) membership: member identities as portable identifiers; (c) relationships: membership and follow edges; (d) permissions: roles and moderation powers - and ask what is missing, what is unnecessary, and which items are deal-breakers if absent. Map their requirements onto (a)-(d), keeping three things visible rather than folded away: additions the list does not contain, unknowns (items they cannot classify without trying), and non-account state (requirements that live outside any account's data, such as moderation services, app views, relay membership, bots and integrations). Mark every mapped requirement as raised unprompted or confirmed-after-prompt; the comparison between the two is descriptive, not causal identification.

**D - Destination assumption (R1).** Present the three framings and ask which matches their situation: (a) a named real destination with its documented properties; (b) a hypothetical destination with named commitments (continuity, pricing stability); (c) the bounded-artifact framing - the offer carries artifacts and the destination is their own problem. The chosen assumption is part of the record, not background.

**E - Stated willingness (R4).** Under their chosen destination assumption: would you rehearse a copy in an admin-only test space if it cost about an afternoon of your attention? Would you consider a real move? Record the answers verbatim and label them stated intention under a hypothetical offer - a prior, not behavior (R4).

**F - Attribution probes (small-n mode from R2).** After any refusal or interest: "What is that mainly about?" The verbal report is the attribution instrument; any later variant contrast is corroboration, not an independent measurement. Reported reasons are preserved as reported; a prompt comparison is not causal identification.

## Decline and refusal recording

If the conversation itself is declined, or a rehearsal offer is refused: record the offer text verbatim, the stated reason verbatim, and the confound class it most suggests - timing, trust, platform mismatch, offered preservation scope, or other/none stated. A refusal with no recorded reason is logged as uninterpretable, never as absence of demand; no necessity inference about missing machinery is drawn from any refusal pattern (R3).

## Variant handling at small n

At n=1, probes only; no variant assignment. If further conversations happen, alternate which destination-assumption framing is presented first, and do not counterbalance framings within one conversation unless the respondent raises both unprompted (within-subject exposure invites demand effects; the within/between choice stays deferred per R2). Report and contrast are never reported as independent measurements.

## Reporting back

The conversation is written up as an issue-#5-style account or a new research note: handle or anonymous per the respondent's choice, verbatim quotes only as reviewed by them, and every statement labeled observed behavior, stated intention, or the interviewer's interpretation. Social-state elements this instrument cannot observe are named as unobserved, not excluded (R5).

## Limits

A single conversation is an anecdote about one administrator; hypothetical-offer bias applies to section E (R4); the probes inherit the framing they ask about (R2); refusal counts identify nothing by themselves (R3); nothing here schedules, recruits for, or obtains consent for any conversation - fielding it is a separate decision with a named administrator's consent, and the human side (finding and talking to that administrator) belongs to the project owner.
