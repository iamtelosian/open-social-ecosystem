# W1b first-conversation instrument: one administrator's present workflow and required preservation scope

Status: proposed instrument, 2026-11-07 - held for review before any use; not fielded, and no conversation has been run or scheduled under it. Source: issue #2 rounds 4-7 (thelumenfield, iamtelosian); revised 2026-11-07 under iamtelosian's review on PR #6 (reported-vs-observed distinction, fixed hypothetical offer, small-n labeling). Requirements: research/w1b-instrument-requirements.md (R1-R5), with the R2 deviation stated in "Small-n status" below. Nothing here is a measurement, a recruitment, or consent obtained.

## Reporting taxonomy (applies to every recorded item)

Every statement in the conversation record carries exactly one of four labels:

- **Reported behavior** - the respondent's account of what they did or do, past or current. Testimony, not observation.
- **Directly observed action** - something the interviewer directly observes the respondent do, with what counts as the action defined in advance and its source and date recorded. A conversation under this instrument is not an observation session; this label is expected to go unused, and it is never assigned by inference from testimony.
- **Stated intention** - what the respondent says they would do under a stated condition.
- **Interpretation** - the interviewer's reading, including any mapping of requirements onto the wedge card's categories.

No label upgrades without the evidence: reported behavior never becomes observed action in the write-up, and the respondent's account of their role, community, or problem remains their report, not independent verification of it.

## Purpose and claims boundary

One completed conversation under this instrument can establish:

- Whether a real administrator reports a current exit problem - their report of their present situation, not a hypothetical, and not independent verification of the role or the problem.
- What preservation scope that administrator states as required - the first beneficiary test of the wedge card's (a)-(d) proposed requirement, which the card carries as unsettled. Requirements raised unprompted are recorded separately from requirements confirmed only after the checklist is shown, so the checklist cannot silently supply the answer.
- What they say they would do under a stated, recorded hypothetical offer (stated willingness under R4).

It cannot establish adoption, payment, behavioral follow-through, that the required scope is buildable at acceptable cost, or anything about administrators in general. n=1 is one administrator's account, reported under this instrument's framing.

## Consent wording (read first, verbatim or adapted to the channel)

"This is a research conversation about how your community's content and member state is currently kept, and what a move would need to preserve. It asks for no live data, no access to your community or accounts, and no commitment from either side - talking does not sign you up for anything, and declining anything costs nothing. You can skip any question or stop at any point. Notes are attributed by a handle you choose, or kept anonymous. Nothing about you or your community is published without you reviewing it first."

## Question set

**A - Present workflow (reported behavior, not hypothetical).** Current platform and role as they describe them; community size band; how they report keeping a copy of the community's content and member state today, if at all; the last time they report needing or wishing for a copy, and what they report happened. Record what they report doing, labeled reported behavior, distinct from what they say they value.

**B - Problem currency (reported).** Is there a current reason to move? If not current: when was the last one, and what triggered it? A past problem is dated reported evidence; only a current reported problem makes this conversation a beneficiary test rather than another retrospective account. The existence and currency of the problem are the respondent's report throughout.

**C - Required preservation scope, elicited open first.** Before showing any list: for your community, what would a copy need to carry for you to call it complete? What would you be unwilling to lose? Record the unprompted requirements verbatim. Only then show the wedge card's proposed scope - (a) content: posts, media, moderation records; (b) membership: member identities as portable identifiers; (c) relationships: membership and follow edges; (d) permissions: roles and moderation powers - and ask what is missing, what is unnecessary, and which items are deal-breakers if absent. Map their requirements onto (a)-(d) (mapping is interpretation, labeled as such), keeping three things visible rather than folded away: additions the list does not contain, unknowns (items they cannot classify without trying), and non-account state (requirements that live outside any account's data, such as moderation services, app views, relay membership, bots and integrations). Mark every mapped requirement as raised unprompted or confirmed-after-prompt; the comparison between the two is descriptive, not causal identification.

**D - Destination framing and the fixed offer (R1).** Present the three framings and ask which matches their situation: (a) a named real destination with its documented properties; (b) a hypothetical destination with named commitments (continuity, pricing stability); (c) the bounded-artifact framing - the offer carries artifacts and the destination is their own problem. The chosen framing is part of the record, not background. Choosing (a) verifies nothing about the named destination: its properties are recorded as documentation claims to be checked, not as facts established by the respondent's choice.

Before any willingness question in E, the exact offer is recorded in writing as part of the record, with every field filled:

- offered preservation scope, itemized;
- known omissions - items this offer cannot carry or cannot observe;
- destination properties: documented properties under (a), named commitments under (b), or an explicit "unknown" under (c);
- estimated attention burden and any cash burden to the respondent;
- assistance included, if any, with its limits - nothing in the offer implies a working group-migration service or any project assistance commitment;
- hypothetical status, stated to the respondent: this is a hypothetical offer for research purposes, not a service that exists and not a promise of one.

If these fields cannot be specified before the conversation, section E is omitted and the conversation stays with present needs (A-C). An undefined offer is not a willingness measure.

**E - Stated willingness (R4).** Under the recorded offer, at the attention and cash burden recorded in D: would you rehearse a copy in an admin-only test space at that recorded burden? Would you consider a real move under the recorded offer? The questions quote the recorded burden verbatim and introduce no new figure, so the question cannot silently substitute a different offer. Record the answers verbatim and label them stated intention under the recorded hypothetical offer - a prior, not behavior (R4).

**F - Reason probes.** After any refusal or interest: "What is that mainly about?" The answer is recorded as the respondent's stated reason, preserved as reported. A stated reason is not a causal attribution, and no contrast between framings is drawn from these answers (see small-n status below).

## Decline and refusal recording

If the conversation itself is declined, or a rehearsal offer is refused: record the offer text verbatim, the stated reason verbatim, and the confound class it most suggests - timing, trust, platform mismatch, offered preservation scope, or other/none stated. A refusal with no recorded reason is logged as uninterpretable, never as absence of demand; no necessity inference about missing machinery is drawn from any refusal pattern (R3).

## Small-n status and the R2 deviation

This instrument is exploratory verbal-report elicitation at n=1; it does not satisfy R2's two-variant decomposition. One chosen framing plus probes cannot separate the tool from the destination, and alternating which framing is presented first in later conversations is not itself a one-factor design. Accordingly this instrument makes no variant assignment, no counterbalancing, and no comparative or otherwise attributable signal. Any comparative instrument is a separate, separately specified design with its own review. This section is an explicit, recorded deviation from R2 as written in research/w1b-instrument-requirements.md, kept here so the instrument and the requirement remain reconcilable.

## Reporting back

The conversation is written up as an issue-#5-style account or a new research note: handle or anonymous per the respondent's choice, verbatim quotes only as reviewed by them, and every statement labeled per the reporting taxonomy above. Social-state elements this instrument cannot observe are named as unobserved, not excluded (R5).

## Limits

A single conversation is an anecdote about one administrator; everything it yields about behavior and problems is reported, not observed; hypothetical-offer bias applies to section E (R4), compounded if any offer field is thin; reason probes yield stated reasons that inherit the framing they ask about, not causes (R2 deviation stated above); refusal counts identify nothing by themselves (R3); nothing here schedules, recruits for, or obtains consent for any conversation - fielding it is a separate decision with a named administrator's consent, and the human side (finding and talking to that administrator) belongs to the project owner.
